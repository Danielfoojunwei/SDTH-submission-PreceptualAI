# Plan: RadioJEPA + WirelessJEV — a physics-structured radio world model

## The main thing
Build **RadioJEPA**, a world model of the radio scene built from radio physics (architecture name: SCENE-JEPA), and let **WirelessJEV** plan with it. The goal is to beat RadioMap under the same trust contract, on any device and layout we can test.

## Context
- **Repo:** `Danielfoojunwei/SDTH-submission-PreceptualAI`. Canonical tip is `codex/jepa-pilot-fastloop`, a linear stack of 15 Codex branches that includes deck commit `998fe37`.
- **Today:** the shipped system is RadioMap + WirelessJEV. Codex's FleetJEPA got 4.3% fewer misses than RadioMap but failed qualification. A matched supervised model ties or beats it in every isolated test.
- **Paper (arXiv 2610.39302, Balestriero & LeCun):**
  - JEPAs trained on *good views* (simultaneous observations of one scene) converge to one shared representation, even with separate encoders per modality.
  - Next-step prediction is a *bad view*, needing about 42× more data.
  - The recipe uses SIGReg for anti-collapse, and frozen encoders read by linear heads.
  - Code: `github.com/galilai-group/prh-proof` (licence to check).

## Roles and naming (deck contract kept)
- **RadioJEPA:** the world model. It is the deck's "proposed JEPA model", now with the SCENE-JEPA architecture.
- **RadioJEV:** the archived E1–E5 experiment only.
- **WirelessJEV:** the decision layer.
  - The JEV reads the mission (`native/intent_policy.py`) and turns it into cost/SLA weights.
  - The planner queries RadioJEPA's action-conditioned predictor for candidate actions: roam, band, MLO link, MCS, admission, scan.
  - The trust gate (`gate_repair/gate.py`, `production_qualification`) vetoes. It stays outside the model and is never trained by it.
- **RadioMap** (`native/radiomap.py`): the default, fallback and independent baseline until RadioJEPA qualifies.
- **Attribution test:** the same WirelessJEV planner and gate, swapping only the world model.

## Why the current JEPA fails (from the code and Codex's own audits)
1. **Bad views.** The pretext task predicts the future 6-scan window from the past one (`fleetwm/data.py:scan_context`). The h=0 target equals the input, and the targets contain route geometry already supplied as input.
2. **Collapse.** Only EMA plus a variance hinge guard against it. Effective rank is about 7/48 (and 1.6–1.9 for semantic JEPA), and future-latent prediction is worse than a constant (`docs/REPRESENTATION_COLLAPSE_AUDIT.md`).
3. **Competing objectives.** One latent serves both the pretext and the forecast head, with no projector (`fleetwm/model.py`).
4. **Truth leakage.** The semantic-JEPA teacher reads simulator truth SNR (`semantic_jepa/training.py:229-236`).
5. **Unbounded residual on RadioMap.** `map_mean + 15·head` pays both models' costs, about 2.8× the work.
6. **One device.** Every client is a BE200 at 15 dBm. Data is RSSI only, just 16.6% of link histories are heard, and there is no real data.
7. **Train/deploy mismatch.** Training uses idealised 1 s scans; deployment uses delivered 5 s scans with partial pilots.
8. **Objective ≠ decision.** Training targets absolute SNR, while control uses differences, and `reactive_rescue` overrides learned uncertainty.
9. **Tiny, single-law data.** 6,144 rows from 32 sites, generated by the same law RadioMap already encodes. Evaluation uses 8 sites × 60 s.

## First principles RadioJEPA is built on
**Electromagnetics**
1. **Sparse multipath.** A channel is a sum of paths (delay, angle, Doppler, gain), so the latent is a set of scatterer/path tokens.
2. **Frequency structure.** Geometry is band-invariant while loss and penetration grow with frequency. The 2.4/5/6 GHz views are related by a *known operator*, and the thermal floor kTB gives the operator between 20/80/160/320 MHz bandwidths.
3. **Reciprocity.** Uplink ≈ downlink, so AP-side and client-side measurements are free positive pairs.
4. **Coherence scales.** Coherence time, bandwidth and distance (λ/2 ≈ 2.5 cm at 6 GHz; shadowing decorrelates over metres, per Gudmundson) set the masking. Masking inside one coherence interval is copying.
5. **Superposition, causality, passivity.** Interference adds; a path's delay is at least d/c; no energy is gained. These become hard constraints.
6. **Phase and Doppler.** They reveal sub-wavelength motion and occluded movers (NLOS through racks).

**Analog front end and devices**
7. **Device = nuisance operator.** RSSI offset and quantisation, AGC, noise figure, antenna pattern, CFO/STO/phase noise and IQ imbalance are modelled as an operator. "Any device" means learning its inverse, with known invariances (phase sanitisation, cross-antenna products) built in.

**Information and statistics**
8. **Fading is a distribution.** Predict per-link fading statistics (Rician K, Nakagami m) and the shadowing variance. This gives calibrated outage probability, replacing the fixed 5 dB spreads.
9. **The decision quantity is rate and outage, not SNR.** Heads predict P(packet or command miss | action) via PHY/PER curves (`native/phy.py`).
10. **Value of information.** A scan costs 90 ms of airtime, so the planner picks the scan or pilot with the highest expected information gain per airtime cost (active sensing).
11. **A minimal sufficient latent per timescale.** The slow scale (geometry, minutes) is shared by the fleet; the mid scale (blockage and movers, seconds) serves roaming and AP choice; the fast scale (fading, ms) serves MCS and pilots.

**Medium access and networks**
12. **Airtime conservation.** Busy fractions per channel sum to at most 1, and CSMA contention follows the interference graph. This constrains load and admission forecasts.

**Geometry, motion and symmetry**
13. **Symmetries.** Treat the scene as SE(2)/SE(3)-equivariant and APs/robots as permutation-invariant sets. Attention replaces today's mean-pooling.
14. **Known kinematics.** Planned robot routes give future positions, and human motion follows priors.
15. **The fleet is a distributed aperture.** Many robots observe the same scatterers: good views and a synthetic aperture at once.

**Compute**
16. **Real-time is a physical budget.** Inference must finish within the control-loop deadline. The design uses anytime inference, and the total work of backbone plus heads must not exceed the current stack.

## Component audit: what changes because we are radio-native
Nothing is kept because a vision or NLP paper used it. Each component below is replaced by its physics-matched version, and the generic version survives only as an ablation baseline.

### Why SIGReg (isotropic Gaussian) is not the default
- SIGReg pushes the whole embedding toward an isotropic Gaussian: unbounded, unimodal, every direction equivalent.
- The radio scene is none of those:
  - **phases** are circular (S¹);
  - **path gains** are sparse (a few strong paths);
  - **powers** are positive, log-scaled and censored;
  - **positions** are bounded by the building;
  - **materials** are discrete;
  - **airtime shares** lie on a simplex (they sum to at most 1);
  - **interference** is heavy-tailed and impulsive.
- Only the large-scale shadowing field is physically Gaussian, and only in dB (log-normal shadowing).
- Even the paper proves uniqueness only for second-order anti-collapse, as population results. It does not show SIGReg is optimal anywhere, let alone in radio.
- **Replacement: a Physics-matched Prior Regulariser (PPR).**
  - Each latent factor's marginal is matched to its physical prior. SIGReg's characteristic-function (Epps–Pulley) test works against any target distribution, so this is a generalisation of SIGReg rather than a different method.
  - The targets per factor:
    - von Mises/uniform for phases;
    - spike-and-slab or Laplace for path gains;
    - Gaussian in dB for shadowing;
    - uniform over the floor plan for positions;
    - categorical for materials;
    - Dirichlet for airtime.
  - Decorrelation is applied only between factors physics says are independent (geometry ⟂ fast fading), not across every dimension.
- **Collapse is mostly prevented by the operator itself.** The physics operator has to re-render real observed views, so a collapsed latent can't satisfy it. That makes the regulariser secondary.
- **Ablation ladder:** none + operator, then SIGReg, then VICReg, then PPR. The best performer is decided by measurement, not assumed.

| Euclidean ℝᵈ latent | Mixes circles, sets, simplices and positive scales | **Product-manifold latent:** ℝ²/ℝ³ positions, S¹ phases, ℝ₊ log-powers, simplex airtime, variable-size token sets |

Gaussians under-predict tails, the likely cause of every worst-decile failure | **Distribution-family heads** (Rician-K, Nakagami-m, log-normal σ, burst-state model), or direct **CDF/quantile at the MCS decision thresholds**, with tail-weighted calibration |
| L1/L2 latent & forecast loss | Errors are multiplicative (dB) but superposition is additive in linear power (mW); circular phase loss is wrong in ℝ | **Domain-correct losses:** dB loss for large-scale, mW for interference sums, 1−cos for phase, **Tobit/interval likelihood** for censored and integer-quantised RSSI (the pretext currently ignores censoring) |
| Absolute-SNR target | Control consumes differences and outage, not SNR | **Decision-focused loss:** Brier/CRPS on outage at the MCS table thresholds, plus predict-then-optimise regret |
| LayerNorm on encoder outputs | Erases absolute power level, which decides decodability | **Scale-preserving normalisation** with a separate log-power bypass channel; normalise only dimensionless factors |
| Sin/cos positional features | Not tied to propagation | **Wavelength-relative Fourier features per band**, log-distance encodings, attention bias proportional to predicted path coupling |
| Mean-pooled fleet context | Ignores who interferes with whom | **Interference-graph attention:** edges weighted by coupling (hearing range, CSMA contention, adjacent-channel leakage); permutation-invariant over APs/robots |

| Fixed-step MLP dynamics | Scans are asynchronous; shadowing correlates over *distance* (Gudmundson: exponential); fading follows the Doppler spectrum (Jakes) | **Continuous-time state-space dynamics:** an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck prior in distance travelled for shadowing, a Jakes/AR prior for fading, event-driven updates on irregular scans |

| Point-estimate inference | The scene is partially observed (16.6% of histories heard) | **Amortised Bayesian filtering:** a posterior over the scene latent, updated per scan; RadioMap's Bayesian strength kept |
| Pixel-free JEPA dogma (no measurement-space loss) | Radio measurements are low-dimensional and physically meaningful, unlike pixels | **Hybrid:** latent prediction for high-dimensional CSI; measurement-space likelihood through operators for RSSI/RTT/ACK |
| Uniform sampling of training rows | Service is decided by rare deep fades, handovers and interference bursts | **Tail-aware sampling and importance weighting** of handover moments, fades and bursts |

| Float model at inference | Must meet the control-loop deadline on edge hardware | **int8 operators, anytime inference, compute budget enforced as a test** |

## SCENE-JEPA architecture
1. **Front-end inversion encoders** per device or sensor family: RSSI (with RadioMap's censored Tobit likelihood), CSI, FTM, PER/ACK counters, and camera (DINOv3). A new device trains only its own encoder.
2. **Shared scene latent:** scatterer tokens (position, material, velocity), a large-scale field initialised from the log-distance prior, and slow/mid/fast scales.
3. **Physics-operator predictor:** frequency, bandwidth, geometry, Doppler, superposition and device operators, plus a small learned residual. It is **action-conditioned** on channel, power, MCS, roam and MLO link, so it can answer counterfactuals.
4. **Coherence-scaled masking** of whole bands, APs, antennas, devices, regions and time spans beyond coherence.
5. **Loss:**
   - cross-view latent prediction for CSI, plus measurement-space likelihoods through operators (Tobit/ordinal RSSI, RTT, ACK);
   - domain-correct distances (dB / mW / circular);
   - the physics-matched prior regulariser (PPR), with SIGReg and VICReg as ablations;
   - hard constraints for causality, passivity and airtime.
   The self-supervised part uses no simulator truth. Dynamics use continuous-time OU/Jakes priors, and inference is amortised Bayesian filtering on a product-manifold latent.
6. **Heads** on the frozen latent, each with a separate projector, trained with weak supervision from the protocol (ACK/NACK, block ACK, retries, MCS, RTT, BSS load):
   - current bias + route increment;
   - outage probability per action, fed to `closed_loop/control.py:OutageUtility`;
   - fading statistics;
   - load and airtime;
   - value of information for scans.

## Native WirelessJEV integration

### One interface, two world models
- `WorldModel.query(scene_state, candidate_actions, horizon, timescale) → outcome distributions`. Outcomes are outage/miss probability, rate, latency and silence, each with calibrated uncertainty.
- Implemented by `RadioJEPA` and by a `RadioMapAdapter`, which wraps today's forecasts with fading-free spreads. The planner sees only this interface, so the attribution test is a configuration switch.
- The trust gate checks each head's calibration online. When a head fails its check, the gate falls back to the RadioMapAdapter for that head only, not the whole model.

### How the JEV runs on the world model
1. **Mission → cost.** The JEV mission reader (`native/intent_policy.py`) compiles the mission into a cost over predicted outcomes. Example: "pick lane 3, ≤0.1% missed commands, never silent >2 s" becomes weights and chance constraints. The mission is **never** a JEPA view, so untrusted text stays out of the representation.
2. **Planning in latent space (MPC).**
   - The JEV rolls out candidate action sequences through the action-conditioned predictor using CEM or MPPI over a small discrete set.
   - Each decision runs at the timescale it acts on: fast for MCS/pilot, mid for roam/band/MLO link/scan, slow for route and admission.
   - It replans every control tick under a bounded compute budget, keeping the best plan so far if time runs out.
3. **Chance-constrained selection.** Choose the cheapest plan with P(SLA violation) ≤ the mission bound, then send it to the trust gate.

### WirelessJEV component audit: every sub-component questioned
| Component (current) | Problem in radio | Radio-native replacement |
|---|---|---|
| **Mission reader** (`native/intent_policy.py`): text → gate thresholds | Thresholds can't express "never silent >2 s" or per-command criticality; free text is untrusted | Typed **mission spec**: costs plus **signal temporal logic** constraints (e.g. G(silence ≤ 2 s), P(miss) ≤ 10⁻³). A deterministic, validated compiler; any language-model parsing is optional and checked against the schema. Text never reaches actuation |
| **Roam score** = mean best-band SNR over 0–5 s − load penalty (`native/sim.py:403-456`) | SNR isn't service. It ignores handover cost, tails and airtime | **Expected goodput / outage integral** per action, minus a **mechanism-specific switching cost** |
| **`roam_gain_db` hysteresis** | A hand-tuned stand-in for uncertainty and switching cost; causes ping-pong | **Optimal stopping / expected utility** under the calibrated predictive distribution; hysteresis emerges from the cost instead of being set |
| **Trust gate z = gain/√(σ²cur+σ²best)** | Assumes Gaussian, *independent* errors. Errors are correlated through the shared scene, and tails are heavy | Gate on the **joint predictive distribution of the difference**, using **conformal prediction** (distribution-free coverage) calibrated per site/device, with **adaptive conformal** updates under drift |
| **Reactive fallback −75 dBm / ≥8 dB** | dBm thresholds don't carry across devices (RSSI offsets, AGC) | Device-inverted **SINR/outage thresholds**, calibrated per device family |
| **`reactive_rescue` overwrites confidence** | Discards learned uncertainty wholesale | Only per-head fallback to the RadioMapAdapter when that head's conformal coverage check fails |
| **Load penalty** | A proxy; ignores CSMA contention and MLO | **Airtime model:** expected throughput from busy fraction, contention graph and link aggregation (airtime on the simplex) |
| **Per-robot independent decisions** | Many robots roaming to the same "best" AP overload it (herding), and their interference couples them | **Fleet-level coordination:** capacity-aware assignment, staggered roams, interference-aware joint plan (mean-field approximation at large fleet size) |
| **Fixed handover outage** | Real outage depends on mechanism: 802.11r FT, OKC, full re-auth, MLO link switch with no reassociation, Wi-Fi 8 seamless mobility | **Outage distribution per mechanism**, learned from protocol timestamps; the planner prefers MLO link switches over reassociation |
| **MCS by sampling** (Minstrel-style) or fixed | Probing wastes airtime; blind to predicted fades | **Model-based MCS:** choose from the predicted SINR distribution × PER curves (`native/phy.py`); probe only when value of information is high |
| **MLO EMLSR/STR policy** as fixed mode | Ignores per-link forecasts and transition delays | Link selection and transitions planned by the action-conditioned model, including **duplicating critical commands across links** (STR) when P(miss) exceeds the bound |
| **Fixed scan cadence** | 90 ms of airtime per scan, done blindly | **Value-of-information scans**, plus free information: 802.11k neighbour reports, passive beacon listening, 802.11v BTM hints |
| **Admission by threshold** (`admission_sim.py`) | Ignores airtime conservation and tails | **Chance-constrained admission:** admit only if P(SLA violation for all flows) ≤ ε under the airtime constraint |
| **All commands treated alike** | Stop and e-stop commands matter more than telemetry | **Criticality-aware scheduling:** EDCA access classes (AC_VO for control), deadline-aware queueing, redundancy for critical commands |
| **Fixed-period control loop** | Wastes compute in calm periods and reacts late to fades | **Event-triggered replanning** on latent surprise, scan arrival or SLA-risk crossing, with anytime MPC within the deadline |
| **No safety envelope** | A wrong forecast can drive a robot into a dead zone | **Connectivity control-barrier function:** never take actions that push P(silence > bound) above ε; a guaranteed fallback (stay on link / slow / stop robot) always exists |
| **Learning from the controller's own logs** | Selection bias: we only see outcomes of actions we took | **Off-policy evaluation with logged propensities**, plus small safe exploration for calibration |
| **Promotion statistics:** paired tests over 8 sites | Underpowered; ignores robot and site clustering | **Hierarchical (site → robot) models**, pre-registered power analysis, **sequential testing** with alpha spending, tail endpoints (worst decile, silence) as primary |
| **Explanations:** numeric logs | Operators can't act on them | Scene-token causes plus the counterfactual of the rejected alternative |

### A. Writable from existing evidence
**A1. Prediction Is Not Service: When a Radio World Model Beats the Map on Forecast Error but Not on Missed Commands** (ready)
Learned radio world models are usually judged by forecast error. Across [N] native 100-robot warehouse simulations, a JEPA-style forecaster lowered 1–5 s route-change RMSE against a Bayesian censored radio map at 8 of 8 sites, yet raised command loss at 6 of 8. We trace this to the control interface: forecast differences cancel constant errors, max-band averaging hides tails, and fixed spreads override learned uncertainty. We argue that radio world models should be qualified on service endpoints under an independent trust contract, and we release the replay audit.

**A2. Bad Views Collapse Radio Representations: Next-Scan Prediction as a Degenerate Wireless JEPA** (ready + one ablation)
Following the Platonic Representation results for JEPAs, we show that temporal next-scan prediction in Wi-Fi behaves as a *bad-view* objective. The targets overlap their inputs and leak route geometry. Learned representations concentrate to an effective rank of about 7 of 48, and future-latent prediction is worse than predicting a constant (MSE ratio 1.8–2.0×). Replacing the views alone, with everything else held fixed, restores rank to [r] and improves transfer by [x].

**A3. Censored by Design: Bayesian Radio Maps from Top-K Wi-Fi Scan Reports** (ready)
Commodity clients report only their strongest K access points, so missing readings are not missing at random. We present RadioMap, a per-BSS Bayesian model combining log-distance terms, rack obstruction and random Fourier features, fitted with censored (Tobit) EM and a held-out discrepancy variance. With fleet-level priors it is a strong, hard-to-beat baseline for learned forecasters, and we report where it fails.

**A4. Wi-Fi 7 Multi-Link Operation versus Private 5G for Warehouse Robot Fleets** (ready after re-validating against the latest artifacts)
We compare intent-aware Wi-Fi 7 MLO with predictive roaming against private 5G across [3] interference conditions with 100 robots. Wi-Fi achieves [17×] fewer missed commands in realistic conditions and loses in [condition]. All results come from simulation, with confidence intervals and the condition-level limits stated.

### B. RadioJEPA: the representation
**B1. RadioJEPA: A Physics-Structured Joint-Embedding World Model of the Indoor Radio Scene** (Phases 1–2, 5; flagship)
We introduce SCENE-JEPA. View-specific encoders map RSSI, CSI, FTM, protocol counters and images into a shared latent of scatterer tokens and a large-scale field.
